How a posted need becomes a confirmed volunteer

The engine is deterministic and explainable — it never guesses, never emails on its own, and never shares contact details until a coordinator confirms. Every step below is traced to the code merged in PR #5.

1Start here: needs don't match, roles do

A posted need on its own produces nothing

Matching runs against roles you define inside a need, not the need itself. A verified need with no roles shows an empty panel. Two preconditions must both hold:

① The need's status is verified or recruiting  ·  ② at least one role exists on it

site/lib/matching/types.ts:2 · site/lib/matching/data.ts:30

A role carries the hard requirements — skills, work mode, district, dates, hours per week, minimum experience, qualifications — plus soft preferences (preferred specialties, prefer-local, missions) and a headcount. Requirements are what the engine tests; preferences only affect ordering.

3How a volunteer lands in a bucket

Each volunteer runs through roughly twenty checks. Every check returns pass, fail, or unknown, and the worst result decides the bucket.

Ready for invitation

every check = pass

Invitable. The panel shows the full reasoning and an Invite volunteer button.

Needs confirmation

any check = unknown
(and none fail)

Not ruled out — just missing an answer. Each unknown carries the exact question to ask.

Not currently suitable

any check = fail

A real conflict: dates, capacity, travel area, or an explicit decline.

The rule that makes this trustworthy

An unknown never satisfies a requirement. Old registration answers that can't establish a fact are stored as null rather than guessed. A "can help remotely" checkbox proves willingness, not remote-only, so work mode stays unknown until someone confirms it.

6How candidates are ordered

Within a bucket, sorting runs through this tuple in order — each tier only breaks ties left by the one above it. Fully deterministic, so the list never reshuffles between page loads.

  1. More matching specialtiesCounted against the role's preferred specialty tags.
  2. Expressed interest, then mission matchSomeone who asked about this exact need outranks a general mission fit.
  3. Preferred districtOnly when the role sets prefer-local.
  4. Fewest invitations in the last 14 daysSpreads load instead of repeatedly asking the same people.
  5. Least recently contactedRotates fairly through the register.
  6. Stable hash of role + revision + volunteerA deterministic final tiebreak, so equal candidates keep a fixed order.

Tiers 4 and 5 are the fairness mechanism: without them the same well-documented volunteers would win every role forever. engine.ts:116

7The invitation clock

An invitation is a time-boxed reservation, not a commitment. The slot is held while it is live, and released the moment it lapses.

  direction LR

  queued --> sent: worker delivers
  queued --> cancelled: coordinator cancels
  sent --> accepted: volunteer proceeds
  sent --> declined: volunteer declines
  sent --> expired: 48h elapses
  sent --> cancelled: coordinator cancels
  accepted --> confirmed: coordinator confirms
  accepted --> expired: 72h elapses
  accepted --> cancelled: coordinator cancels
  confirmed --> [*]: completed or withdrawn
  declined --> [*]
  expired --> [*]
  cancelled --> [*]

48 hours to respond

From queueing. While live, the slot counts as occupied so the same place isn't offered twice.

72 hours after accepting

Accepting extends the clock, giving you time to confirm with both sides before it lapses.

Role edits invalidate

Editing a role bumps revision. Any invitation issued against the old requirements can no longer be confirmed.

Re-checked at send time

The worker re-runs eligibility before sending and cancels the email if anything changed while queued.
